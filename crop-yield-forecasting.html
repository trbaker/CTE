Module 1 · ArcGIS Field Mapping

Explore the farm with GIS

What is GIS? Geographic Information Systems (GIS) let us layer different kinds of spatial data — soil type, elevation, water flow — on top of each other to find patterns invisible to the naked eye. ArcGIS is the industry standard used by the USDA, NASA, and thousands of farms worldwide.

Module 2 · Drone Flight Planning

Plan an autonomous survey mission

Why drones? Satellites revisit the same spot every 5–16 days and can't fly below clouds. Agricultural drones (UAVs) fly at 100–400 ft, capture centimeter-level imagery, and can be scheduled any day. They carry multispectral cameras that see wavelengths invisible to the human eye — perfect for detecting crop stress before you can see it.

Module 3 · Remote Sensing Analysis

Read the crop's vital signs with NDVI

NDVI formula: (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red). Healthy plants absorb red light for photosynthesis and strongly reflect near-infrared (NIR). Stressed or dying plants reflect more red and less NIR. NDVI ranges from −1 to +1. Values above 0.5 indicate healthy, dense canopy. Below 0.3 suggests bare soil or struggling crops.

Module 4 · Yield Forecasting

Build your prediction model

How agronomists predict yield: They combine multiple data layers — soil type, plant population, NDVI readings, weather (GDDs — Growing Degree Days), and historical yields — using regression models. ArcGIS has built-in spatial statistics tools that can run these predictions field-by-field.
